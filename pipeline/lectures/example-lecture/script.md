# Introduction

[slide: 01.png]
Welcome back, everyone. [pause 0.6s] Today we begin one of the most consequential
chapters in modern history: the French Revolution. By the end of this lecture you
should be able to explain not just *what* happened in 1789, but *why* the old order
collapsed so quickly.

Let's start with a question. [pause 0.8s] How does a kingdom that had stood for
centuries fall apart in a matter of months?

## Scene: The Three Estates

[slide: 02.png]
To understand the Revolution we first have to understand French society on the eve
of it. France was divided into three estates. The First Estate was the clergy. The
Second Estate was the nobility. And the Third Estate — roughly ninety-seven percent
of the population — was everyone else.

[broll: paris-streets.mp4]
Picture the streets of Paris in the 1780s: crowded, hungry, and increasingly angry.
The people who fed the nation paid nearly all of its taxes, yet had almost no voice
in how it was governed.

# Financial Crisis

[slide: 03.png]
The monarchy was effectively bankrupt. Years of war — including French support for
the American Revolution — had drained the treasury. [pause 0.5s] When the king tried
to tax the nobility, they refused, and the crisis became unstoppable.

# Conclusion

[slide: 04.png]
So when we ask why the Revolution happened, the answer is not a single cause but a
collision of them: social inequality, financial ruin, and a government that could no
longer adapt. [pause 0.7s] Next time, we'll follow the events of 1789 themselves.
